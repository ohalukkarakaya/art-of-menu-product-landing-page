In short

A QR menu for restaurants is a digital menu guests open by scanning a code at their table, organized by course with photos, prices and allergens. ArtOfMenu adds automatic language for visitors, your own domain and, on the Order plan, table ordering that sends each order straight to the waiter's phone during busy service.

A long menu, easy to read on a phone

Restaurant menus are long. Starters, mains, sides, desserts, a wine list and a weekend brunch section all compete for attention. On a phone, a menu split into clear sections lets guests jump straight to what they are deciding on and come back without losing their place.

Photos matter most for dishes guests cannot picture from the name. A photo of the house ragù or the sharing platter answers “how big is it?” before anyone has to ask the waiter.

Allergens on every dish

Each item carries its allergen information, shown in the guest's own language. A guest with a nut allergy can check the dessert list before calling anyone over, and your staff make fewer trips to the kitchen to ask.

Keep the data accurate. When a recipe or a supplier changes, update the item that day. Because the menu is live, the change reaches the next table at once.

Tourists read your menu in their language

Near a station, a museum or the old town, many lunch tables are visitors. The menu opens in the language the guest's phone uses, while prices stay in your local currency with your country's tax display. A family from Lisbon can order the regional dish instead of the one they happen to recognize.

Table ordering for the lunch rush

At 12:30 every table wants to order at once. On the Order plan, guests send their order from the menu and the waiter sees it instantly on their phone, with a notification. Staff spend their time carrying plates and checking on tables instead of standing at each one with a notepad.

Table ordering works alongside your kitchen routine and checkout. ArtOfMenu does not replace your POS or payment terminal.

Smart features for busy kitchens

On the Smart plan, recipe-based stock marks a dish sold out as soon as an ingredient runs out, so nobody orders the last sea bass twice. Sales reports show which dishes sell and which barely move, which is a good starting point for your next menu change.

Smart also suggests the item most often ordered with each dish at your branch, and it can set tomorrow's prices each evening inside the minimum and maximum you choose.

Frequently asked questions

What should a restaurant look for in a QR menu?

Clear sections for each course, a photo and allergens for each dish, quick loading on mobile data and a way to change prices instantly. If you serve tourists, automatic language matters. If service gets busy, table ordering takes pressure off the floor team.

Can guests order and pay through the QR menu?

On the Order plan, guests send orders from the menu to the waiter's phone. Payment stays with your existing terminal or POS, since ArtOfMenu is not a payment system. Your team takes payment exactly as it does today.

How do I show allergens on a digital menu?

Add allergen details to each item in ArtOfMenu. Guests see them next to the dish, in their own language. Review them whenever a recipe or supplier changes, because the menu is only as accurate as the details behind it.

Will the QR code change when I update the menu?

No. The QR code on the table stays the same. You update dishes, prices and photos in the menu itself, and every new scan shows the latest version, so you never pay to reprint table stands.

How much does a restaurant QR menu cost?

$12 per branch per month on the Menu plan, $35 on Order with table ordering and $69 on Smart. Yearly billing costs 10 months' price. In Europe the plans cost €9, €29 and €59.
