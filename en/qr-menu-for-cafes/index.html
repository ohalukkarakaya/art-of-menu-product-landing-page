A QR menu for cafés and coffee shops.

A café QR menu lets regulars and newcomers see every drink, size and pastry on their phone while they queue or sit down. ArtOfMenu keeps it current as your specials change with the seasons.

Get started See how it works
In short

A QR menu for a café is a digital drinks and food menu that guests open by scanning a code at the counter or table. ArtOfMenu shows photos and allergens and opens in the guest's language. On the Smart plan, it also moves hot drinks up when the weather turns cold and suggests the pastry most often ordered with a coffee.

Built for the morning rush

Between 8 and 9, the queue sets the pace of your day. If people can read the menu on their phone while they wait, they reach the counter knowing what they want, and the barista spends less time explaining the difference between a cortado and a flat white.

Place one code by the queue and one on each table. Both open the same menu, so the guest who sits down after ordering can look up the pastries for a second round.

Seasonal drinks without reprinting

Pumpkin spice in October, iced matcha in June, a chestnut latte for six weeks in winter. Cafés change their drinks list more often than most businesses. With a QR menu you add the new drink with a photo, set its price and take it off when the season ends. The code on the table stays where it is.

Allergens matter here too: oat, soy and almond milk, nuts in the granola, gluten in the banana bread. Each item shows its allergen details in the guest's language.

When it gets cold, the menu follows

On a cold, rainy Tuesday, hot chocolate should be the first thing guests see. ArtOfMenu's weather-aware order handles this on its own. When it gets cold, hot drinks move up, and cold drinks and items about to run out move down.

Only the order changes during the day. Prices stay where you set them.

Pairing coffee with something sweet

A latte on its own is a small ticket. On the Smart plan, when a guest adds a drink, the menu shows the item most often ordered with it at your branch. If your regulars take an almond croissant with their cappuccino, the next guest sees that suggestion too.

Bakeries and cafés with a counter

If you sell over a counter, put one code by the till and one on each table; both open the same menu. On the Smart plan, recipe-based stock helps on busy mornings: when you run out of oat milk, every drink made with it shows as sold out on its own, so nobody pays for a drink you cannot make.

Frequently asked questions

Do cafés really need a QR menu?

Not every café does. It helps most when your drinks list changes with the seasons, when queues build up in the morning or when many guests are visitors. A small, fixed menu on a chalkboard may be all a tiny espresso bar needs.

Can I put the QR code on the counter instead of tables?

Yes. The code simply opens the menu, so it works on a counter sign, a window sticker or each table. You can use both: one near the queue and one on every table.

How do I add a seasonal drink to a QR menu for a cafe?

Add it to the menu with a photo, price and allergens, and it appears on the next scan. When the season ends, remove it. You never reprint the QR code.

What does weather-aware menu order do?

When it gets cold, hot drinks move up the menu, and cold drinks and items about to run out move down. During the day only the order of items changes, never the price.
