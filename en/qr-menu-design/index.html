A stylish QR menu design starts with your photos.

A beautiful QR menu comes from three things: good photos, readable text and a layout that stays out of the way. ArtOfMenu provides the clean layout, and your photos and words do the rest.

Get started See how it works
In short

Good QR menu design means guests can read the menu on a small screen, see what each dish looks like and recognize your business. ArtOfMenu uses one clean, consistent layout for every menu, filled with your product photos, item descriptions and allergen details, on your own domain. It has no ready-made themes or custom code uploads.

Why design affects what guests order

On a phone, guests skim. They look at photos first, then names, then prices. A clear layout makes it easy to compare two dishes. A cluttered one makes guests settle for the first familiar thing. Items that never get noticed never get ordered.

You can test this. Move a dish up its section or give it a better photo, then compare its orders over the next few weeks. On the Smart plan, sales reports show what sells, and the menu order adjusts itself to sales and to what guests look at.

Photos do most of the work

You do not need a professional shoot. A recent phone, a clean table near a window and a few minutes before service is enough for most dishes.

  • Shoot in daylight rather than under warm kitchen lights
  • Use the same angle and background within a section so the list looks calm
  • Show the real portion on the plate you actually serve it on
  • Leave an item without a photo rather than using a stock image

Text that reads well on a phone

Keep item names short and descriptions to one line. Say what is in the dish and how it is cooked. “Charred leeks, hazelnut, brown butter” tells a guest more than a paragraph about the chef's inspiration.

Put allergen information on the item itself, where guests expect to find it. Group sections the way people order: drinks, small plates, mains, desserts.

Your brand, on your own domain

When a guest scans, the menu opens on your business's own domain. The address bar shows your name, which matters to guests who hesitate before tapping an unfamiliar link. Beyond that, your brand lives in your photos, your item names and the way you write about your food.

What ArtOfMenu does not do

To set expectations clearly: ArtOfMenu does not offer a theme gallery or a design service, and you cannot upload your own HTML or CSS. Every menu uses the same clean layout, built for reading on a phone. If you want a fully custom look, a bespoke website is the better route.

Design that adapts during the day

On the Smart plan, the order of the menu responds to the weather. When it gets cold, hot drinks move up, while cold drinks and items about to run out move down. The layout guests know stays the same, and the most fitting items rise to the top.

Frequently asked questions

Can I customize the design of my QR menu?

You control the content that shapes the look: photos, item names, descriptions and sections. The layout itself is the same clean design for every ArtOfMenu menu. There are no themes to choose from and no custom HTML or CSS.

What makes a good QR code menu design?

Readable text at phone size, photos for the dishes that need them, one-line descriptions, allergens on each item and sections that match how guests order. A shorter list of strong items usually looks better than a long one.

Do I need a designer to make a beautiful digital menu?

Not with ArtOfMenu. The layout is already designed. What makes the difference is the quality of your photos and how clearly you describe each item. An afternoon with a phone camera before service is a good place to start.

How should the printed QR code look on the table?

Keep the code large and high-contrast so it scans in low light, add a short line such as “Scan for the menu” and put your colors around the code rather than on it. Test it with a few phones before printing a batch.

Does the menu show my business name?

Yes. It opens on your own domain, so your name is in the address bar from the moment the menu loads. Guests who hesitate before tapping an unfamiliar link can see at a glance that the menu is yours.
