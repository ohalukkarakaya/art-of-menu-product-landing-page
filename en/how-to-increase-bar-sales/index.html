How to increase bar sales, one round at a time.

To increase bar sales, speed up service, build a drinks menu that steers guests toward better-margin drinks and control stock so pour costs stay honest. Each of these can be measured on a normal Friday night.

Get started See how it works
In short

Bar sales grow when guests order more rounds, trade up to better drinks and stay a little longer, while pour costs stay under control. The main levers are a focused drinks menu, faster service, a happy hour that fills quiet hours, premium suggestions, food that suits drinks and accurate stock. ArtOfMenu's table ordering and recipe-based stock help with speed and sold-out drinks.

Measure the night before changing it

Track three figures for each type of night: guests, average spend per guest and pour cost. Pour cost is the cost of what you poured divided by drink sales, and it tells you whether a busy night actually made money.

Work it out by category. Draft beer, wine, cocktails and spirits each have their own normal range, and the gap between what your count says you used and what you sold is where money leaks.

Build a drinks menu that sells

Listing every spirit you stock makes for a long read and a slow decision. Guests choose faster when the menu has a clear structure: signature cocktails, classics, beer by style, wine by the glass and a short spirits section.

Put your signatures first and give each one a line of ingredients. Signatures are where you set the price, because nobody can compare them with the bar next door. Keep the list short enough that every drink can be made well at speed, and review it each season. Retire drinks that sell slowly and need ingredients nothing else uses.

Speed up every round

On a busy night, sales are limited by how fast you take orders and pour. Every minute a group waits to order is a minute without the next round.

  • Batch the prep for your top cocktails before service
  • Set up stations so the most-ordered bottles are within reach
  • Let table guests order without having to wave for attention
  • Clear empty glasses quickly and ask about the next round as you do

Run a happy hour that pays

Happy hour should fill hours that would otherwise be quiet. If 6 pm is already busy, a discount there only gives margin away. Choose drinks that still earn a healthy margin at the lower price, keep the offer simple and end it before your natural peak.

Pair it with food. A plate of something salty gives a table a reason to stay for a second drink. Check the rules where you operate first, since some areas restrict drink promotions.

Suggest premium spirits naturally

When a guest orders a gin and tonic, a server who asks “our house gin, or the one from the local distillery?” turns a basic drink into a premium one with a single question. Train staff on two or three premium options per category and let them taste each one, so the suggestion sounds like advice rather than a script.

Sell food that keeps people at the table

Salty, shareable food keeps a table ordering: fries, nuts, flatbreads, sliders. It also slows the drinking down, which keeps the evening pleasant for everyone in the room. Put two or three bar snacks right next to the drinks rather than on a separate page.

Control stock and pour cost

Count spirits weekly, by bottle and estimated fraction, and compare usage with sales. Use jiggers or measured pourers for every drink, including the ones your bartender has made a thousand times.

Watch the drinks that depend on fresh ingredients. When limes or mint run out mid-service, guests keep ordering what you cannot make. With recipe-based stock on ArtOfMenu's Smart plan, one ingredient running out marks every drink made with it as sold out on its own, and sales reports show what each night sold.

Give people a reason to come on a Tuesday

Quiet nights need their own plan: a quiz, a local band, a tasting night with a nearby brewery or a weekly food special. Pick one, run it on the same night every week for long enough to build a habit and tell your regulars about it in person.

Frequently asked questions

How do I increase beer sales in a bar?

Keep draft lines clean and beer fresh, describe each beer by style on the menu, offer a tasting flight for undecided guests and rotate one guest tap so regulars have something new. Pair beers with salty snacks and offer a second pint when you clear the first glass.

How can I increase liquor sales in a restaurant?

Suggest a drink with every food order, by name. Train servers on a few wine and cocktail pairings, show drinks next to dishes on the menu and offer a premium spirit when a guest orders a mixed drink.

Does happy hour actually increase bar sales?

It can, if it fills quiet hours and the discounted drinks still earn a margin. It loses money when it discounts hours that would be busy anyway. Check local rules too, since some areas restrict drink promotions.

How can I serve drinks faster on busy nights?

Batch cocktail prep before service, set up stations around your top sellers, keep glassware stocked and let table guests order from their phones. On ArtOfMenu's Order plan, those orders reach staff phones instantly.

How often should I count bar inventory?

Weekly for spirits and wine, plus a full count at the end of every month. Compare what you used with what you sold. Large gaps point to over-pouring, unrecorded comps or breakage.
