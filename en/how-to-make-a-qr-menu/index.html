How to make a QR menu, fast.

To make a QR menu, put your menu online, link a QR code to it and place that code on your tables. With ArtOfMenu you sign up in the panel and build the menu there, or put the PDF you already have online.

Get started See how it works
In short

Making a QR code menu takes four basic steps: put the menu online, check prices and allergens, print a QR code that links to it and place the code where guests sit. With ArtOfMenu you can put your existing PDF online the same day on any plan, or build the live menu with items, photos and allergens in the panel. The printed code never changes; you update the menu behind it.

Before you start: what you need

Four things are enough to go live.

  • Your current menu, as a PDF or on paper
  • A list of allergens for each item
  • A few phone photos of your dishes, if you have them
  • Your business's own domain, if you want the menu to open on it

QR menu maker or QR code generator?

A QR code generator only makes the code: a square that points to a link. If that link is a PDF file, guests get a document they have to pinch and zoom, and every change means uploading a new file.

A QR menu maker builds the menu itself, with items, photos, allergens and languages, and you edit it online behind a code that stays the same. ArtOfMenu works this way. If you connect it in ChatGPT, ChatGPT can help turn a photo or PDF of your menu into items, and you approve the changes in the panel.

Printing and placing the code

Test the code before you print a batch. Scan it with two or three different phones, in the lighting of your own dining room at night. Avoid glossy laminate that reflects ceiling lights, and on outdoor tables use weatherproof stickers or stands that will not blow over.

Updating prices and dishes

When a price changes, edit it in the menu, and the next scan shows it. When a dish comes off the menu, remove it. On the Smart plan, recipe-based stock marks items sold out on their own when an ingredient runs out, so you do not have to remember mid-service.

Make your QR menu in six steps

  1. Gather your current menu

    Find the latest PDF, the one with this month's prices. If you only have a design file, export it to PDF first.

  2. Add your items

    Sign up in the panel and add your sections and items. If you want something online today, upload the PDF as a PDF menu while you build.

  3. Check prices and allergens

    Go through each item, confirm the price, add allergen information and fix anything that changed since the old menu was printed.

  4. Add photos where they help

    Start with your top sellers and the dishes guests cannot picture from the name.

  5. Print the QR code

    Print it large enough to scan from a seat, in dark ink on a light background, with a short line like “Scan for the menu”.

  6. Place it on every table

    Use table stands, stickers or tent cards. Put one near the entrance or queue too, so guests can read the menu before they sit.

Frequently asked questions

How do I create a QR code for my menu?

Put your menu online first, then create a QR code that points to it. With ArtOfMenu, you sign up in the panel, add your menu or upload your PDF, and print the code the panel gives you for your tables.

Can I just link a QR code to my PDF menu?

You can, but guests then have to zoom into a document on a small screen, and every change means replacing the file. A menu built item by item reads properly on phones and lets you edit items one at a time. ArtOfMenu lets you start with the PDF and switch to the live menu when it is ready.

How long does it take to make a QR menu?

With ArtOfMenu, you can put your existing PDF online the same day. Building the live menu with photos and allergens for every item takes longer; the optional ChatGPT integration can help turn your PDF into items, which you approve in the panel.

What size should a QR code be on a table?

Print a test sheet at a few sizes and scan each one from a seat in your dining room's evening light. Choose the smallest size that scans on the first try with every phone you test, then go a little bigger.

Do I need to reprint the QR code when the menu changes?

No. The QR code on the table stays the same. You change items, prices and photos in ArtOfMenu, and the next guest who scans sees the update. Stands and stickers you print once can stay on the tables.
