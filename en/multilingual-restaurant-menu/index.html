A multilingual restaurant menu that opens in the guest's language.

A multilingual restaurant menu shows each guest the same dishes and prices in their own language. With ArtOfMenu, the QR menu reads the language of the guest's phone and opens in it automatically.

See how it works
In short

A multilingual restaurant menu presents your dishes in several languages so visitors can order without guessing. ArtOfMenu's QR menu detects the language set on the guest's phone and opens in it, with allergen information shown in that language and prices in your local currency. It is included on every plan, from $12 per branch per month.

Why language changes what tourists order

A visitor who cannot read the menu orders the one dish they recognize, or points at the next table. When the menu is in their language, they can try the regional dish, ask fewer questions and add the dessert they would otherwise have skipped. Your staff spend less time translating at the table during a full service.

Language picked from the phone

The guest does not have to look for a language option. The menu opens in the language the phone already uses. A German couple sees German, a family from Madrid sees Spanish, and your regulars see the menu as they always do.

The printed code is the same for everyone, so one table stand serves every guest.

Allergens guests can actually read

Allergens are where language matters most. A guest with a sesame allergy needs to understand the warning exactly. With ArtOfMenu, allergen information appears with each item in the language the menu opens in.

Prices in your currency, set up for your country

ArtOfMenu can be set up for 216 countries. Currency, tax display and price rounding are set up per country, so prices look the way they should where you operate, whatever language the menu opens in.

Where it matters most

Old towns, beach strips, ski villages, streets around stations and museums, and any neighborhood where hotels outnumber offices. Restaurants in Spain, Italy, France, Germany and Turkey see this every summer. In the US and UK, it is often the downtown places near the sights, plus anywhere with international students or business travelers.

Tips for a menu that works in any language

Automatic language gives guests a clearer menu when the original is written simply.

  • Keep dish names short and describe ingredients plainly
  • Keep the local name of a signature dish and explain it in one line
  • Skip puns and wordplay in descriptions, since they rarely make sense in another language
  • Check allergen details every time a recipe changes

Frequently asked questions

How does a multilingual QR menu know the guest's language?

It reads the language setting of the guest's phone. When the guest scans the code, ArtOfMenu opens the menu in that language, so the guest does not have to choose anything first.

Is the multilingual menu included in every plan?

Yes. Automatic language is part of the Menu plan, so it is in Menu, Order and Smart. The Menu plan costs $12 per branch per month, or €9 in Europe.

Are allergens shown in the guest's language?

Yes. Allergen information is part of each item and appears in the language the menu opens in. Keep it up to date when recipes or suppliers change, because the menu is only as reliable as the details you enter.

Which currency do guests see?

Your local currency. ArtOfMenu sets currency, tax display and price rounding per country, across 216 countries, so a tourist sees the same price your regulars see. The language changes with each guest, while the prices stay those of your business.

Is a multilingual menu useful outside tourist areas?

Yes, in any city with international students, business travelers or communities that prefer another language. If even a few tables a night struggle with your menu, automatic language helps them order with confidence.
